Some settings belong to the community, others belong to **you**. The ones on
this page are yours: you set them once and they apply everywhere you are signed
in with the same account.

You find them in the dashboard of any community under **Settings** — and just
the same in your account area at `account.pukalani.app` under **Profile**. It is
one and the same setting, whichever way you reach it.

## Two-factor sign-in

Under **Settings → Security** sits the **Two-factor** card: a six-digit code
from an authenticator app on your phone, on top of your password. Someone who
knows your password still does not get in.

### Setting it up

::steps{level="4"}

#### Scan the QR code

After clicking **Set up**, a QR code appears. Scan it with your authenticator
app. No scanner at hand? The same secret is printed below for typing in
manually.

#### Confirm the code

Enter the code your app shows. Only that proves the app is really set up.

#### Write down the recovery codes

Pukalani then shows a list of recovery codes. **They are shown this one time
only.** Write them down somewhere safe — they are your way back in if you lose
your phone. Each code works exactly once.

::

From then on, signing in asks for your password **and** a code. A recovery code
always works in place of the code from the app.

### What to know about it

::warning
**When you sign in with Google, Google verifies your factors and your Pukalani
code is not requested.** The second factor applies to password sign-in. If you
use Google to sign in, the security of your access depends on your Google
account settings — set the second factor up there in that case.
::

::note
**Turning it off asks for a valid second factor**, not for your password: a
code from the app or a recovery code. Whoever removes the second factor should
show that they still have it. That a recovery code counts here is deliberate —
otherwise a lost phone would be a dead end.
::

## Time zone

Under **Settings → General**, the **Time zone** card sets the time zone used to
display dates and times.

- The default is **Automatic (browser)** — the time zone of your device applies,
  exactly as before.
- Otherwise you search for your zone in the field and pin it. Right below the
  field the effect is spelled out: *Now: 04:24 in Europe/Berlin*. A setting
  whose consequence you would only notice tomorrow should be checkable today.

Two places where deliberately nothing changes:

- **Relative times** such as "5 minutes ago" stay as they are — a difference has
  no time zone.
- **Plain dates** without a time mean the same day wherever you are.

::note
**The daily limit on upvotes does not follow your time zone.** It counts one
fixed calendar day for everybody — otherwise switching zones would hand out a
second allowance the same evening. More on that under
[Discussions](/anleitung/produkte/diskussionen).
::

## Your name for mentions

Also under **Settings → General**: the short name others use to mention you in
posts and comments — `@yourname`.

- **It belongs to your account**, not to a single community: the same person is
  called the same everywhere, and two accounts cannot hold the same name at the
  same time. Whoever claimed it first keeps it.
- **You do not have to fill anything in.** Pukalani suggests a name based on
  your display name; you are mentionable from day one.
- **Allowed are** lowercase letters, digits and underscores, 3 to 24 characters
  — an underscore may not sit at the start or the end. Some names are reserved
  (`pukalani`, `team`, `admin` among them).
- **You can change it every 30 days.** The card tells you when the next change
  is possible.
- **Old mentions stay intact.** If you rename yourself, the old name stays
  reserved for you and keeps pointing at you in posts already written.

How mentioning itself works is described under
[Discussions](/anleitung/produkte/diskussionen).

## Your activity

In your account area on `account.pukalani.app`, **Profile → Activity** shows
what you have done — across all of your communities. The page has two parts:

- **Account and security** shows what happened to your account: profile
  changed (with the name of the field, such as "Profile picture"), password
  changed or reset through the link from the email, email address verified,
  two-factor turned on or off, and wrong two-factor codes.
- **Below that are your communities** with your posts, comments, RSVPs and
  enrolments.

What you should know:

- **You only see your own.** No other member sees this list, and it never
  shows anything from others.
- **An account event carries the place** it came from (city and region), as far
  as it can be determined — not an IP address. If it names a place you have not
  been to, change your password and check where you are signed in under
  **Settings → Sessions**.
- **It shows THAT something changed, not WHAT.** Neither the old nor the new
  value is stored anywhere in this list.
- **Ordinary sign-ins and sign-outs are not listed** — the important entries
  would drown in them. Where you are signed in right now is shown in the
  session list.
